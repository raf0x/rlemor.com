# Creature Studio, possible standalone iOS product

## Product hypothesis

A separate child-first creative playground inspired by The Synthetic Menagerie.
It is **not** a museum feature or part of the current website release.

The central promise: "Draw something. Bring it to life. Play with it."

## Two complementary modes

1. **Meet and play**: A curated set of original, animated characters with simple touch interactions. Kids can rotate and move them, recolor materials, trigger safe actions (hop, glide, dance, spin, curl), and arrange play scenes.
2. **Draw and bring to life**: A finger-paint canvas with optional outlines and shapes, undo/redo, filled colors and stamp tools. Kids tap "Bring to life", and the drawing becomes a playable moving character in a controlled toy environment.

## Build an honest first prototype

Do not promise that an arbitrary drawing becomes a perfect rigged 3D asset in seconds. A much more credible, fun initial implementation:

- Start with a 2D/2.5D puppet transformation. The child's original stroke colors and silhouette remain recognizable.
- Support a small catalog of riggable drawing templates first: frog, fish, bird, imaginary blob and octopus.
- Map supplied contours to predefined joints and movement patterns. Preview the results before confirming.
- Let the child repaint, rename and record short offline animation clips for local family sharing.
- Build a standalone iOS app with a native touch UI and appropriate rendering. Explore full open-ended AI 3D conversion only after testing reliability, performance, cost, visual fidelity and child safety.

## Child-first design and safety

- Start without accounts, public profiles, chat, freeform social sharing, or targeted advertising.
- Prefer local/offline drawings, images and saved creations.
- Protect external links, purchases, exports and settings behind an appropriate grown-up gate.
- If any cloud-based generation is added later, establish age-appropriate content moderation, consent/data practices, latency limits, ongoing cost controls and a reliable fallback.
- Review current Apple App Store Kids Category guidelines, applicable child privacy requirements and accessibility before launch. Do not presume that the museum's website privacy or performance behavior is appropriate for a children's product.

## Milestone proposal

1. Sketch an original tactile app experience around the two modes.
2. Prototype one existing creature with five delightful interactions.
3. Prototype one drawing-to-life workflow from finger strokes into a moving character.
4. Test with children and parents on real devices before expanding the cast or starting a monetization strategy.

Success criteria: kids discover the interaction without reading, feel ownership over their creation, and voluntarily return to make another.

This is a concept brief, **not** a claim that the iOS app exists or is App Store-ready.
